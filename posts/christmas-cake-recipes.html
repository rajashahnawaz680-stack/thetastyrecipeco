A Christmas cake table should feel like the sweet finish to the whole day: something tall and showy in the middle, something chocolate for the chocolate lovers, and something spiced or fruity for everyone else. This roundup gathers twenty-five Christmas cake recipes — yule logs, layer cakes, bundts, pound cakes and sticky puddings — all made for a holiday table. Bake one showpiece ahead, add a simpler cake the day before, and dessert takes care of itself.

How to plan a Christmas cake table

Pick three cakes, not one: a showpiece like a yule log or a frosted layer cake, a bundt or pound cake that slices easily, and a chocolate cake for the crowd. If the day is busy, lean on cakes that keep well — pound cakes, bundts and spiced cakes taste even better on day two. If you are feeding a crowd, choose cakes that serve from one pan or slice cleanly, and set out small plates so everyone can try more than one slice.

Good to know: Everything is written in American English, and measurements use US customary units — cups, tablespoons, and Fahrenheit — for cooks in the United States. Always follow safe food-handling guidance, and check labels for allergens when cooking for others.

Food safety: egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C) — bake cakes until a toothpick comes out clean and the center is set. Refrigerate cream cheese frosted cakes promptly. Check with a food thermometer.

1. Classic Yule Log Cake (Bûche de Noël)

The classic French Bûche de Noël: a light chocolate sponge rolled around whipped chocolate cream, shaped into a log and dusted with powdered sugar like fresh snow. It slices to show a beautiful spiral, tastes rich but not heavy, and looks like the centerpiece of the whole Christmas table.

Ingredients

  • 4 eggs
  • 3/4 cup sugar
  • 3/4 cup flour
  • 1/4 cup cocoa powder
  • 1 cup heavy cream
  • 8 oz dark chocolate
  • powdered sugar

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Line a 15x10-inch jelly roll pan with parchment paper and lightly grease it.
  2. Whisk the eggs and sugar in a large bowl for 5 minutes, until thick, pale and tripled in volume.
  3. Sift the flour and cocoa powder over the egg mixture and fold gently with a spatula until no streaks remain.
  4. Spread the batter evenly in the pan and bake for 12 minutes, until the sponge springs back when touched.
  5. Turn the warm sponge onto a towel dusted with powdered sugar, peel off the parchment, and roll it up. Let it cool completely, about 30 minutes.
  6. Whip the heavy cream until soft peaks form, then fold in the melted and cooled dark chocolate.
  7. Unroll the sponge, spread the chocolate cream evenly, and re-roll it snugly. Dust with powdered sugar before serving.

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

2. Red Velvet Christmas Cake

Deep red layers with a whisper of cocoa, stacked high under tangy cream cheese frosting. The crumb stays soft and moist thanks to the buttermilk, and the color makes every slice look festive on the plate. It is the cake guests reach for first at a Christmas table.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups flour
  • 1 1/2 cups sugar
  • 1 cup buttermilk
  • 1/2 cup butter
  • 2 eggs
  • 2 tbsp cocoa powder
  • 1 oz red food coloring
  • 8 oz cream cheese

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Grease and flour two 9-inch round cake pans.
  2. Beat the butter and sugar in a large bowl for 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  3. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each, then stir in the red food coloring.
  4. Whisk the flour and cocoa powder together, then add them to the bowl in three parts, alternating with the buttermilk. Mix just until smooth.
  5. Divide the batter between the pans and bake for 28 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.
  6. Cool the layers in the pans for 10 minutes, then turn them out onto a rack and cool completely, about 1 hour.
  7. Soften the cream cheese, spread the cream cheese frosting between the layers and over the top and sides, and smooth it with a spatula.

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

3. White Chocolate Cranberry Cake

Soft vanilla layers studded with tart fresh cranberries and sweet white chocolate, finished with a white chocolate buttercream. The berries pop against the pale crumb, and the sweetness of the chocolate balances their sharp bite. It is a bright, pretty cake for a Christmas dessert table.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 1/2 cups sugar
  • 3/4 cup butter
  • 3 eggs
  • 1 cup white chocolate chips
  • 1 cup fresh cranberries
  • 1 tsp vanilla

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Grease and flour two 9-inch round cake pans.
  2. Melt the white chocolate chips in a heatproof bowl over simmering water, stirring until smooth. Set aside to cool slightly.
  3. Beat the butter and sugar in a large bowl for 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  4. Add the eggs one at a time, then stir in the vanilla.
  5. Fold in the flour in two parts, then fold in the melted white chocolate and the cranberries.
  6. Divide the batter between the pans and bake for 35 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.
  7. Cool the layers for 10 minutes in the pans, then turn out onto a rack to cool completely before frosting with white chocolate buttercream.

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

4. Eggnog Pound Cake

A dense, buttery pound cake steeped in eggnog and nutmeg, baked in a bundt pan and finished with an eggnog glaze. Every slice is rich, moist and warmly spiced, with the familiar flavor of the holiday drink in cake form. It keeps beautifully for days.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups flour
  • 2 cups sugar
  • 1 cup butter
  • 5 eggs
  • 1 cup eggnog
  • 1 tsp nutmeg
  • 1 tsp vanilla

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 325°F. Grease and flour a 10-cup bundt pan.
  2. Beat the butter and sugar in a large bowl for 4 minutes, until pale and fluffy.
  3. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition.
  4. Whisk the flour and nutmeg together, then add them to the bowl in three parts, alternating with the eggnog. Stir in the vanilla and mix just until smooth.
  5. Pour the batter into the bundt pan and smooth the top.
  6. Bake for 1 hour 10 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean and the top is golden.
  7. Cool the cake in the pan for 15 minutes, then turn it out onto a rack to cool completely before drizzling with eggnog glaze.

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

5. Spiced Apple Cider Cake

Warm cinnamon and nutmeg run through this soft cake, made with apple cider reduced down to a concentrated, syrupy flavor and studded with diced apples. It bakes up moist and fragrant, like apple pie in cake form, and needs nothing more than a dusting of powdered sugar.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups flour
  • 1 1/2 cups sugar
  • 1 cup apple cider
  • 1/2 cup butter
  • 3 eggs
  • 2 apples
  • 1 tsp cinnamon
  • 1/2 tsp nutmeg

Directions

  1. Pour the apple cider into a small saucepan and simmer over medium heat for 10 minutes, until reduced by half. Let it cool completely.
  2. Heat the oven to 350°F. Grease and flour a 9-inch round cake pan.
  3. Beat the butter and sugar in a large bowl for 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  4. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each.
  5. Fold in the flour, cinnamon and nutmeg in two parts, alternating with the cooled reduced cider, then fold in the diced apples.
  6. Pour the batter into the pan and bake for 40 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.
  7. Cool the cake in the pan for 10 minutes, then turn it out onto a rack. Dust with powdered sugar before serving.

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

6. Chocolate Peppermint Cake

Dark chocolate layers with a cool peppermint lift, frosted in chocolate buttercream and showered with crushed candy canes. The mint cuts through the richness just enough, and the red and white crunch on top makes it look unmistakably Christmas. Serve it at room temperature for the softest crumb.

  • 3/4 cup cocoa powder
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1/2 cup oil
  • 1 tsp peppermint extract
  • 1/2 cup crushed candy canes

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Grease and flour two 9-inch round cake pans.
  2. Whisk the flour, sugar and cocoa powder together in a large bowl.
  3. Add the eggs, milk, oil and peppermint extract, and beat until the batter is smooth, about 2 minutes.

  5. Cool the layers in the pans for 10 minutes, then turn them out onto a rack to cool completely, about 1 hour.
  6. Frost the cake with chocolate buttercream, spreading it between the layers and over the top and sides.
  7. Sprinkle the crushed candy canes over the top just before serving.

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

8. Molasses Spice Cake

Dark, sticky and deeply spiced, this old-fashioned cake tastes of ginger, cinnamon and molasses in every bite. The crumb stays moist for days, and a cloud of whipped cream on top is the perfect cool contrast. It is the kind of cake that fills the house with Christmas smell while it bakes.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups flour
  • 1 cup molasses
  • 1 cup hot water
  • 1/2 cup butter
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 tsp ginger
  • 1 tsp cinnamon
  • 1 tsp baking soda

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Grease and flour a 9-inch square cake pan.
  2. Stir the baking soda into the hot water until dissolved, then stir in the molasses.
  3. Beat the softened butter in a large bowl for 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  4. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each.
  5. Fold in the flour, ginger and cinnamon in two parts, alternating with the molasses mixture, until just combined.
  6. Pour the batter into the pan and bake for 35 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.
  7. Cool the cake in the pan for 10 minutes, then turn it out onto a rack. Serve warm or at room temperature with whipped cream.

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

12. Vanilla Bean Christmas Cake with Cranberry Frosting

Classic vanilla bean cake with a tender, fragrant crumb, dressed in a festive pink cranberry buttercream. Real vanilla seeds speckle the layers, and the tart cranberry frosting balances the sweetness beautifully. It is an elegant cake that tastes like Christmas in its simplest, prettiest form.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups flour
  • 1 1/2 cups sugar
  • 3/4 cup butter
  • 4 eggs
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 vanilla bean
  • 1 cup cranberry sauce

Directions

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

13. Cinnamon Roll Cake

All the flavor of a cinnamon roll baked into a soft, tender cake, with ribbons of cinnamon and brown sugar swirled through every slice. A cream cheese glaze drips over the top, just like the real thing. It is best served warm, when the swirl is gooey and the kitchen smells like a bakery.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups flour
  • 1 1/2 cups sugar
  • 1 cup butter
  • 4 eggs
  • 1 cup milk
  • 3 tbsp cinnamon
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar
  • 4 oz cream cheese

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Grease and flour a 9x13-inch baking pan.
  2. Stir the cinnamon and brown sugar together in a small bowl for the swirl.
  3. Beat the butter and sugar in a large bowl for 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  4. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each.
  5. Fold in the flour in two parts, alternating with the milk, until just combined.
  6. Pour the batter into the pan, sprinkle the cinnamon sugar over the top, and swirl it through with a knife.
  7. Bake for 40 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean and the top is golden.
  8. Cool the cake for 15 minutes, then top with the cream cheese glaze before serving.

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

14. Hot Chocolate Fudge Cake

An extra-fudgy chocolate cake that tastes like a mug of hot chocolate in solid form, with melted chocolate chips running through the crumb. It is dark, moist and deeply chocolatey, and it is at its best served warm with a spoonful of whipped cream melting over the top.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 2 cups sugar
  • 1 cup cocoa powder
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 cup hot water
  • 1/2 cup oil
  • 1 cup chocolate chips
  • 1 tsp vanilla

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Grease and flour a 9-inch round cake pan.
  2. Whisk the flour, sugar and cocoa powder together in a large bowl.
  3. Add the eggs, oil and vanilla, then stir in the hot water a little at a time until the batter is smooth.
  4. Fold in the chocolate chips until they are evenly distributed through the batter.
  5. Pour the batter into the pan and bake for 35 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.
  6. Cool the cake in the pan for 10 minutes, then turn it out onto a rack.
  7. Serve the cake warm, with a generous spoonful of whipped cream on top.

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

15. Lemon Rosemary Olive Oil Cake

Bright lemon and fragrant rosemary meet in a tender olive oil cake that stays moist for days. The olive oil gives the crumb a soft, almost silky texture, and the herbal note keeps the sweetness in check. Dust it with powdered sugar and serve it with tea or coffee.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups sugar
  • 3/4 cup olive oil
  • 4 eggs
  • 1/2 cup lemon juice
  • 2 tbsp lemon zest
  • 1 tbsp chopped rosemary

Directions

  6. Bake for 40 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean and the top is golden.

22. Brown Butter Pecan Cake

Nutty brown butter and toasted pecans give this cake a deep, toffee-like flavor that regular butter never reaches. The crumb stays soft and rich, and the brown butter frosting doubles down on the nuttiness. It is a warm, cozy cake that tastes like the holidays in every bite.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups flour
  • 1 1/2 cups sugar
  • 1 cup browned butter
  • 4 eggs
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 cup chopped pecans
  • 1 tsp vanilla

Directions

  1. Melt the butter in a saucepan over medium heat, swirling often, until amber and nutty, about 5 minutes. Pour into a bowl and cool for 20 minutes.
  2. Heat the oven to 350°F. Grease and flour two 9-inch round cake pans.
  3. Beat the cooled browned butter and sugar for 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  4. Add the eggs one at a time, then stir in the vanilla.
  5. Fold in the flour in two parts, alternating with the milk, then fold in the pecans.
  6. Divide the batter between the pans and bake for 30 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.
  7. Cool the layers in the pans for 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack to cool completely, about 1 hour.
  8. Frost the cake with brown butter frosting, spreading it between the layers and over the top and sides.

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

23. Mint Chocolate Chip Cake

Cool mint and mini chocolate chips turn a soft white cake pale green and festive, under a mint buttercream. The mint is fresh, not toothpaste-sweet, and the little chips give every slice a chocolate crunch. It is a playful cake that kids and adults both reach for.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups flour
  • 1 1/2 cups sugar
  • 3/4 cup butter
  • 4 egg whites
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 tsp mint extract
  • 1 cup mini chocolate chips
  • green food coloring

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Grease and flour two 9-inch round cake pans.
  2. Beat the butter and sugar in a large bowl for 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  3. Add the egg whites one at a time, then stir in the mint extract and a few drops of green food coloring.
  4. Fold in the flour in two parts, alternating with the milk, then fold in the mini chocolate chips.
  5. Divide the batter between the pans and bake for 28 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.
  6. Cool the layers in the pans for 10 minutes, then turn them out onto a rack to cool completely, about 1 hour.
  7. Frost the cake with mint buttercream, spreading it between the layers and over the top and sides.

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

24. Orange Cardamom Cake

Fragrant cardamom and bright orange make this cake smell like a spice market at Christmas. The crumb is soft and buttery, the orange icing adds a sweet citrus shine, and the cardamom gives it a warm, exotic note that sets it apart from every other cake on the table.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups flour
  • 1 1/2 cups sugar
  • 3/4 cup butter
  • 4 eggs
  • 1/2 cup orange juice
  • 2 tbsp orange zest
  • 1 tsp cardamom

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Grease and flour a 9-inch round cake pan.
  2. Beat the butter and sugar in a large bowl for 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  3. Add the eggs one at a time, then stir in the orange zest and orange juice.
  4. Fold in the flour and cardamom in two parts until just combined.
  5. Pour the batter into the pan and smooth the top.
  6. Bake for 40 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean and the top is golden.
  7. Cool the cake in the pan for 10 minutes, then turn it out onto a rack to cool completely before glazing with orange icing.

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

25. Sticky Toffee Pudding Cake

A dark, moist date sponge soaked in warm toffee sauce, sticky and rich in the best way. The dates melt into the crumb and give it a deep caramel sweetness, and the sauce poured over the top makes every slice glisten. Serve it warm, ideally with a spoon and no apologies.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 1/2 cups chopped dates
  • 1 cup hot water
  • 1 cup brown sugar
  • 1/2 cup butter
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 1 cup toffee sauce

Directions

  1. Put the chopped dates and baking soda in a bowl, pour over the hot water, and let them soak for 10 minutes until soft.
  2. Heat the oven to 350°F. Grease and flour a 9-inch square cake pan.
  3. Beat the butter and brown sugar in a large bowl for 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  4. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each.
  5. Fold in the flour in two parts, then fold in the soaked date mixture with its liquid.
  6. Pour the batter into the pan and bake for 35 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.
  7. Warm the toffee sauce, pour it over the cake, and serve warm.

Food safety: bake until a toothpick comes out clean — egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).

Make-ahead & serving tips

Bake unfrosted layers and bundts up to two days ahead, wrap them well once cool, and frost them the day you serve them so the crumb stays moist and the frosting looks fresh. Bring refrigerated cakes to room temperature for about an hour before slicing so the crumb softens and the frosting cuts cleanly, and use a hot, dry knife wiped between slices for neat pieces. Store cream cheese frosted cakes in the fridge and all other cakes covered at room temperature for up to 3 days, and freeze unfrosted layers for up to a month when you want to get ahead.
