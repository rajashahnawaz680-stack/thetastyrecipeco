Snowball cookies are the quiet stars of every Christmas cookie tray: buttery, nutty, melt-in-your-mouth rounds rolled twice in powdered sugar until they look like fresh snow. This roundup gathers twenty-five Christmas snowball cookie recipes — classic pecan and walnut, chocolate and mocha, peppermint and coconut, fruit and spice, and a few special flavors like matcha and salted caramel. Bake one batch for the cookie exchange, freeze a second for Christmas Day, and keep a jar of powdered sugar handy — the second roll is what makes them snowballs.

How to bake perfect snowball cookies

Chop the nuts fine so the dough holds together and every bite has crunch. Roll the warm cookies in powdered sugar as soon as they come out of the oven, then roll them a second time once they are cool — the first coat melts into the cookie and the second stays snowy white. Bake just until the bottoms are set; snowballs should stay pale, not brown. Let them cool on the pan for a few minutes before moving them, and store them in an airtight container for up to a week, or freeze them for up to a month.

Good to know: Everything is written in American English, and measurements use US customary units — cups, tablespoons, and Fahrenheit — for cooks in the United States. Always follow safe food-handling guidance, and check labels for allergens when cooking for others.

Food safety: the eggs in these cookies are baked through — bake until set and golden. For egg dishes, the USDA safe temperature is 160°F (71°C)..

Classic, nut & cocoa snowballs

The classics that started it all — buttery, nutty rounds rolled twice in powdered sugar until they look like fresh snow.

1. Classic Christmas Snowball Cookies

The original snowball cookie, buttery and tender, with finely chopped pecans folded through a soft shortbread-like dough. Each round bakes up pale and crumbly, then gets rolled twice in powdered sugar so the coat stays thick and snowy white. They melt in your mouth and taste like every Christmas cookie tray you remember.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup butter
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar
  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 cup finely chopped pecans
  • 1 tsp vanilla
  • powdered sugar for rolling

Directions

  1. Set the butter out for about 30 minutes so it softens to room temperature, then place it in a large bowl with the powdered sugar.
  2. Beat the butter and powdered sugar together with a mixer on medium speed for 2 to 3 minutes, until light, fluffy, and pale.
  3. Stir in the flour, finely chopped pecans, and vanilla with a wooden spoon or spatula, mixing just until a soft dough forms and no dry flour remains.
  4. Preheat the oven to 350°F and line a baking sheet with parchment paper.
  5. Scoop tablespoon-sized portions of dough and roll each one between your palms into a smooth ball about 1 inch across, spacing them 1 inch apart on the sheet.
  6. Bake for 15 minutes, until the bottoms are set and lightly golden and the tops stay pale. Do not let them brown.
  7. Let the cookies cool on the pan for 5 minutes, then roll each one gently in powdered sugar while still warm.
  8. Once the cookies are completely cool, roll them a second time in powdered sugar for a thick, snowy coat. Store in an airtight container for up to a week.

3. Walnut Snowball Cookies

Toasty walnuts give these snowballs a deeper, warmer flavor than the classic pecan version, with a delicate crumb that melts away as you bite in. Toasting the nuts first brings out their oils and makes every bite taste richer. Rolled twice in powdered sugar, they look like fresh snow and taste like the holidays.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup butter
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar
  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 cup finely chopped walnuts
  • 1 tsp vanilla
  • powdered sugar for rolling

Directions

  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F. Spread the chopped walnuts on a baking sheet and toast for 5 to 7 minutes, until fragrant, then let them cool completely.
  2. Set the butter out for about 30 minutes to soften, then place it in a large bowl with the powdered sugar.
  3. Beat the butter and powdered sugar together on medium speed for 2 to 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  4. Stir in the flour, the cooled toasted walnuts, and the vanilla, mixing just until a soft dough forms and no dry flour remains.
  5. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper. Scoop tablespoon-sized portions of dough and roll each one into a smooth ball about 1 inch across, spacing them 1 inch apart.
  6. Bake for 15 minutes, until the bottoms are set and lightly golden and the tops stay pale.
  7. Let the cookies cool on the pan for 5 minutes, then roll them gently in powdered sugar while still warm.
  8. Once completely cool, roll them a second time in powdered sugar for a snowy coat. Store in an airtight container for up to a week.

5. Chocolate Snowball Cookies

Cocoa powder turns the classic snowball into a rich chocolate cookie with a dark, tender crumb and the same melt-in-your-mouth texture. Chopped pecans add crunch, and the white powdered sugar coat against the dark cocoa makes them look striking on a cookie tray. They are chocolate and snow in one bite.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup butter
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar
  • 1 3/4 cups flour
  • 1/4 cup cocoa powder
  • 1 cup finely chopped pecans
  • 1 tsp vanilla
  • powdered sugar for rolling

Directions

  1. Set the butter out for about 30 minutes to soften, then place it in a large bowl with the powdered sugar.
  2. Beat the butter and powdered sugar together on medium speed for 2 to 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  3. In a separate bowl, whisk together the flour and cocoa powder, then stir the mixture into the butter along with the finely chopped pecans and vanilla, mixing just until a soft dough forms.
  4. Preheat the oven to 350°F and line a baking sheet with parchment paper.
  5. Scoop tablespoon-sized portions of dough and roll each one into a smooth ball about 1 inch across, spacing them 1 inch apart on the sheet.
  6. Bake for 15 minutes, until the bottoms are set and the cookies hold their shape when touched gently.
  7. Let the cookies cool on the pan for 5 minutes, then roll them gently in powdered sugar while still warm.
  8. Once completely cool, roll them a second time in powdered sugar for a snowy coat. Store in an airtight container for up to a week.

6. Peppermint Snowball Cookies

Cool peppermint extract and crushed candy canes turn the classic snowball into a bright, festive cookie that tastes like Christmas in a bite. The mint keeps the rich butter dough from feeling heavy, and the candy cane pieces sparkle through the snowy sugar coat. They are the prettiest cookie on the tray.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup butter
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar
  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 cup finely chopped pecans
  • 1 tsp peppermint extract
  • 1/2 cup crushed candy canes
  • powdered sugar for rolling

Directions

  1. Set the butter out for about 30 minutes to soften, then place it in a large bowl with the powdered sugar.
  2. Beat the butter and powdered sugar together on medium speed for 2 to 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  3. Stir in the flour, finely chopped pecans, peppermint extract, and half of the crushed candy canes, mixing just until a soft dough forms and no dry flour remains.
  4. Preheat the oven to 350°F and line a baking sheet with parchment paper.
  5. Scoop tablespoon-sized portions of dough and roll each one into a smooth ball about 1 inch across, spacing them 1 inch apart on the sheet.
  6. Bake for 15 minutes, until the bottoms are set and lightly golden and the tops stay pale.
  7. In a shallow bowl, mix the powdered sugar with the remaining crushed candy canes.
  8. Let the cookies cool on the pan for 5 minutes, then roll them in the powdered sugar and candy cane mixture while warm, and roll again once cool for a snowy, sparkling coat.

9. Hazelnut Snowball Cookies

Toasted hazelnuts give these snowballs a warm, Nutella-like depth that makes them taste richer than the classic pecan version. Rubbing off the skins after toasting keeps the flavor smooth and the crumb tender. Rolled twice in powdered sugar, they are buttery, nutty, and quietly elegant.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup butter
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar
  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 cup finely chopped hazelnuts
  • 1 tsp vanilla
  • powdered sugar for rolling

Directions

  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F. Spread the hazelnuts on a baking sheet and toast for 8 to 10 minutes, until the skins loosen and the nuts smell fragrant.
  2. Wrap the warm hazelnuts in a clean kitchen towel and rub them together to remove the skins, then let them cool completely and chop finely.
  3. Set the butter out for about 30 minutes to soften, then place it in a large bowl with the powdered sugar.
  4. Beat the butter and powdered sugar together on medium speed for 2 to 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  5. Stir in the flour, the finely chopped hazelnuts, and the vanilla, mixing just until a soft dough forms and no dry flour remains.
  6. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper. Scoop tablespoon-sized portions of dough and roll each one into a smooth ball about 1 inch across, spacing them 1 inch apart.
  7. Bake for 15 minutes, until the bottoms are set and lightly golden and the tops stay pale.
  8. Let the cookies cool on the pan for 5 minutes, then roll them in powdered sugar while warm, and roll again once cool for a snowy finish.

25. Salted Caramel Snowball Cookies

Caramel bits melt into the buttery dough and a pinch of flaky sea salt on top gives these snowballs a sweet-and-salty finish that makes them impossible to stop eating. The crumb is tender and melt-in-your-mouth, and the powdered sugar coat softens the salt just enough. They are the grown-up favorite.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup butter
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar
  • 2 cups flour
  • 1/2 cup caramel bits
  • 1 cup finely chopped pecans
  • 1 tsp vanilla
  • 1/2 tsp flaky sea salt
  • powdered sugar for rolling

Directions

  1. Set the butter out for about 30 minutes to soften, then place it in a large bowl with the powdered sugar.
  2. Beat the butter and powdered sugar together on medium speed for 2 to 3 minutes, until light and fluffy.
  3. Stir in the flour, caramel bits, finely chopped pecans, and vanilla, mixing just until a soft dough forms and no dry flour remains.
  4. Preheat the oven to 350°F and line a baking sheet with parchment paper.
  5. Scoop tablespoon-sized portions of dough and roll each one into a smooth ball about 1 inch across, spacing them 1 inch apart on the sheet.
  6. Press a small pinch of flaky sea salt onto the top of each ball, using about 1/2 teaspoon total across the batch.
  7. Bake for 15 minutes, until the bottoms are set and lightly golden and the tops stay pale.
  8. Let the cookies cool on the pan for 5 minutes, then roll them gently in powdered sugar while still warm, and roll again once cool for a snowy finish.

Baking & serving tips

Roll the cookies in powdered sugar twice for the snowiest finish, and re-roll them once more before serving if they have been stored. Freeze unbaked dough balls for up to a month, then bake straight from frozen with an extra minute or two in the oven. Mix two or three flavors on one plate for a cookie tray, and keep the cookies in a single layer so the sugar coat stays intact. Leftover powdered sugar keeps well in a jar for the next batch.
