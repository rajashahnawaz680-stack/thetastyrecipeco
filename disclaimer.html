1. Recipe results

Every recipe on this site is written for home cooks and prepared as carefully as we can. Results can still vary depending on ingredients, brands, equipment, oven temperatures, and technique. A casserole that sets perfectly in one oven may need five more minutes in another. Butter, flour, and chocolate differ from brand to brand, and that changes how a dish turns out.

Use your best judgment and adjust to your taste. If a sauce looks too thick, add a splash of liquid. If a cake is browning too fast, lower the oven temperature slightly or cover it loosely with foil. Cooking is a skill, not a formula, and the cook's judgment is part of every recipe.

2. Food safety

Follow safe food-handling practices every time you cook. The basics matter most:

  • Wash your hands and surfaces before you start, after handling raw meat, eggs, or seafood, and before serving.
  • Keep raw and cooked foods separate. Use different boards and knives for raw meat and for ready-to-eat food, and never put cooked food back on a plate that held raw meat.
  • Cook to safe internal temperatures. Meats, eggs, and seafood must reach safe internal temperatures — when in doubt, use a food thermometer and follow USDA safe-temperature guidance.
  • Refrigerate promptly. Do not leave cooked food at room temperature for more than two hours (one hour if the room is warm). Store leftovers in shallow containers so they cool quickly.
  • When in doubt, throw it out. If food smells off, looks unusual, or has been stored too long, do not taste it to check — discard it.

Safe minimum internal temperatures (USDA / FoodSafety.gov) — always check with a food thermometer:

FoodSafe internal temperature
Poultry — chicken, turkey, duck (all cuts, ground, whole)165°F (74°C)
Ground meat and sausage — beef, pork, lamb, veal160°F (71°C)
Steaks, roasts and chops — beef, pork, lamb, veal145°F (63°C), rest 3 minutes
Fish — salmon, cod, tilapia and other fin fish145°F (63°C), or until opaque and flakes easily
Shrimp, lobster, crab and scallopsCook until flesh is pearly and opaque
Egg dishes — quiche, frittata, casseroles with egg160°F (71°C)
Precooked ham (to reheat)140°F (60°C)
Casseroles and leftovers165°F (74°C)

Source: USDA Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart.

These are general guidelines, not a substitute for your local food-safety rules. If you are cooking for young children, older adults, pregnant people, or anyone with a weakened immune system, take extra care.

3. Nutrition information

Where nutrition information is shown, it is an estimate based on typical ingredients and serving sizes. It is calculated from standard nutrition data, not measured in a laboratory, and it may not account for substitutions you make, for the exact brands you use, or for how much of a sauce or dressing actually ends up on the plate.

Nutrition figures are provided for general information only. They are not a substitute for professional dietary advice, and they should not be used to manage a medical condition. If you need precise nutrition information, consult a registered dietitian or another qualified professional.

4. Allergies and dietary needs

Always read ingredient labels and check for allergens before cooking or serving a dish. Recipes on this site may contain any of the major food allergens recognized in the United States — milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, and sesame — and many dishes contain several of them at once.

If you or someone you cook for has an allergy, intolerance, or medical condition, take these steps:

  • Read every ingredient label yourself, even for a product you have bought many times — recipes and formulations change.
  • Check for cross-contact warnings ("may contain" or "made in a facility that also processes") on packaged foods.
  • Ask about ingredients when eating food someone else prepared, and tell your host about any allergy in advance.
  • Consult a qualified professional about substitutions. We cannot guarantee that any recipe is free of a particular allergen, or that a substitution will work the same way.
